# ArmDemo Engineering Log — 2026.8.29

## English

### Goals

- Fix the arm falling back down after it rotates up: it must actively hold its angle instead of relying on motor Brake mode alone.
- Keep the existing 0–90 degree software limits absolutely enforced under the new control mode.

### Completed Work

- Diagnosed the root cause: movement commands ended with `stop()`, setting motor output to `0V`; Brake mode only passively resists, so gravity back-drove the arm.
- Added a position-hold controller to `ArmSubsystem`: `V = clamp(kP·(setpoint − angle) − kD·angleRate + kG·cos(angle − FF offset), ±6V)`.
- Added `holdCurrentPositionCommand()` (built with `Commands.startRun`), registered as the arm's default command so the arm latches and holds its current angle whenever no movement command runs.
- Added three layers of limit protection: hold setpoint clamped to 0–90 degrees, output voltage clamped to ±6V, and a software limit wall that blocks any output pushing further past a limit the arm is already at.
- Preserved all existing `moveUp`/`moveDown` limit checks, button bindings, Brake mode, and elevator behavior.
- Added live-tunable NetworkTables entries for hold gains: `/SmartDashboard/Arm Hold kP`, `Arm Hold kD`, `Arm Hold kG`, and `Arm FF Offset (deg)`; initial values `kP = 2.0 V/deg`, `kD = 0`, `kG = 0`.
- Added angle velocity estimation in `periodic()` and new telemetry: `Arm/AngleVelocityDegreesPerSecond` and `Arm/HoldSetpointDegrees`.
- Fixed a sign bug caught by the new tests: `Math.copySign(output, calibration)` discards the controller's output sign for negative outputs (it would have driven the arm upward against the upper limit while trying to lower); replaced with `output * Math.signum(calibration)`.
- Lowered the maximum arm voltage from `6V` to `3V` at the team's request; this caps both the dashboard-requested movement voltage and the position-hold controller output at `±3V`. Direction handling and the 0–90 degree limits are unchanged.
- Fixed arm oscillation reported on the real robot after reaching the top: the movement commands previously drove a constant `3V` until the angle limit, so the arm arrived at full speed and chattered between the limit wall and the saturated P controller.
- Unified movement and holding onto one position controller whose setpoint slews toward its target at a tunable rate (default `60°/s`), so the arm now decelerates smoothly into the target instead of slamming into the limit.
- Added a `1°` error deadband that gates the proportional term at rest, and enabled derivative damping by default (`kD = 0.02 V·s/deg`) to suppress overshoot; added the `Arm Setpoint Rate (deg/s)` NetworkTables entry and `Arm/HoldTargetDegrees` telemetry.
- Replaced the hard deadband gate with a linear gain taper after on-robot testing showed stutter while rising: the mid-climb tracking lag sat exactly at the gate boundary, so the P term jumped between zero and full output.
- Read the live AdvantageScope trace after the next on-robot test: the rise ran at only 6–8°/s with 1–2° sawtooth at ~0.5s period and 5–8° overshoot at the top. Diagnosis: the 60°/s setpoint ramp saturated the P controller (3V could not sustain that climb speed), leaving the quantized finite-difference velocity and stick-slip unregulated.
- Added a low-pass filter (α = 0.3) to the velocity estimate to remove encoder-quantization spikes feeding the D term, and lowered the default setpoint slew rate to `30°/s` so the controller stays out of saturation while climbing; verified with tests and redeployed.
- After further on-robot testing the team reported the rise still stuttered and noted the original pure-voltage control had always moved smoothly, so the motion strategy was restructured: movement commands now run open-loop on voltage exactly like the original code (full requested voltage mid-travel), tapering linearly to `0.8V` over the last `15°` before each limit so the arm arrives gently. The position controller remains, but only as the default hold command that latches and keeps the arm at its angle once motion ends. This keeps the smooth motion the team validated while fixing both the original falling problem and the arrival chatter.
- Verified from the AdvantageScope trace that the voltage-based motion is smooth in both directions, but the top plateau sat near `80°` with a fine 1–2° sawtooth: the two-sided hold controller was bouncing against the software limit wall (push past 90°, walled to zero, sag back, push again).
- Replaced the hold controller with a one-way hysteresis servo: the arm may sag freely; once it drops more than `1°` below the latched angle a fixed `0.8V` nudge pushes it back, releasing within `0.5°` of the latch so it never overshoots. Strictly one-directional correction cannot oscillate against the wall. Removed the now-unused PID/FF gains, slew machinery, and their NetworkTables entries; 21 tests green, deployed.
- Fixed bottom-of-travel buzzing reported after the next test: the arm's natural rest sits slightly below the calibrated zero, so at the lower limit the nudge kept fighting gravity in an endless push-sag cycle. The hold now outputs nothing when latched within `1°` of the lower limit — gravity itself holds the arm there. Tests updated, all green, redeployed.
- Replaced the hysteresis nudge at the top with a continuous gravity-compensation voltage (`HOLD_VOLTAGE`, live-tunable at `/SmartDashboard/Arm Hold Voltage`): the nudge's on/off nature inherently buzzed ±1° at 90°. Above a `75°` latch the hold outputs the constant voltage and the software limit wall trims it at 90°, so nothing toggles.
- After the next on-robot test the arm no longer stuttered anywhere but sagged slowly at both ends, so `HOLD_VOLTAGE` was raised from `1.4V` to `2.0V`, and the bottom hold was changed from passive silence to leaning on the software zero from below (continuous voltage only while the arm is below `0°`). Both ends now actively rest against their software limits without any toggling chatter. All 28 tests green, deployed.
- The next test session (reviewed from the AdvantageScope buffer after the robot went offline) showed the descent slamming through zero — the approach taper still applied `0.8V` downward while gravity already pulled the arm toward the limit, and the arm ended at `-30°`; the strong 2.0V bottom lean then launched it back above zero and bucked around the boundary. The user also reported chatter at 90° from the same hard-wall bang-bang on the strong top hold.
- Three fixes landed together: the descent taper now runs all the way to `0V` at the lower limit (gravity finishes the approach), the bottom lean uses a gentle dedicated `0.6V`, and the top hold voltage fades linearly to zero over the last degree below 90° so the arm lands on the limit with zero closing speed and never touches the wall. Default `HOLD_VOLTAGE` raised to `2.4V`; all 29 tests green. Deploy pending robot power-on.
- Quantified the remaining top jitter from the AdvantageScope trace (±0.5°, 0.5–1s period, rest point 85°) as encoder-quantization hunting through the 1.2V/deg fade gain; widened the fade band to 2° and added a 100ms low-pass on the angle feeding both fades (`Arm/HoldFilteredAngleDegrees` telemetry added; safety checks still use the raw angle).
- Lowered the elevator movement voltage from `3V` to `1V` for the team's extension testing, then settled on `2V` after on-robot feedback.
- Added an arm–elevator interlock: elevator commands are only scheduled after the arm has reached 90°; the permission holds while the arm rests near the top and clears below `80°`. All 32 tests green; deploys are queued on the auto-deployer while the robot cycles power.
- Reviewed the team's "Arm 与 Elevator 开发" Notion specification against the codebase and produced a gap analysis covering: the dual-motor closed-loop architecture (motor 1 direct, motor 2 reduced), a configurable hold angle (90°/85°), inch-based elevator travel (18–24 in), through-bore encoder adoption, and camera-based pointing at 10–25 m targets.
- With the team, cut the two design/machining hardware items from the plan (the arm motor 2 reduction stage and the lead-screw travel confirmation); this also removes the dual-loop code architecture and the inch parameterization from the software backlog. Remaining hardware purchases: two through-bore encoders (camera setup is long-term); remaining no-hardware software: configurable hold angle and the encoder source switch.
- Implemented both remaining no-hardware items: button 4 now raises the arm to the configurable `TARGET_ANGLE_DEGREES` (default 90°, spec allows 85°) — the motion taper, completion condition, hold fade, and elevator interlock all track the target, while the hard 90° limit wall is unchanged; and added `ArmIOThroughBore` plus the `USE_THROUGH_BORE_ENCODER` switch with placeholder channel/ratio/offset constants for when the encoders arrive. 33 tests green, including a new case verifying an 85° target finishes the command and unlocks the interlock below the hard limit.
- Added seven hold-behavior tests covering sag recovery, setpoint clamping, both limit walls, uncalibrated stop, and encoder direction; all 22 tests and the full build pass.
- Updated the bilingual README with the hold behavior, formula, tuning entries, and a step-by-step on-robot tuning procedure.

### Next Steps

- Purchase two through-bore encoders (arm right side + elevator). On arrival, add the `ArmIO` implementation behind a config switch and make it the authoritative angle source — this removes the startup zeroing dependency and the quantization hunting at the root.
- No-hardware software items when wanted: make the arm hold angle configurable (90°/85° per the spec), and pre-wire the encoder source switch.
- Long term: camera-based pointing at 10–25 m targets for the dynamic demo (separate project, needs camera hardware).

## 中文

### 今日目标

- 修复机械臂向上转动后又会往下掉的问题：必须主动保持角度，而不是只靠电机 Brake 模式。
- 在新的控制方式下，绝对保证既有的 0–90 度软件限位不被超越。

### 已完成工作

- 定位根本原因：运动命令结束时调用 `stop()`，电机输出变为 `0V`；Brake 模式只是被动短路制动，挡不住重力回拽。
- 为 `ArmSubsystem` 新增位置保持控制器：`V = clamp(kP·(目标角度 − 当前角度) − kD·角速度 + kG·cos(角度 − FF 偏移), ±6V)`。
- 新增 `holdCurrentPositionCommand()`（使用 `Commands.startRun` 构建），并注册为机械臂默认命令，只要没有运动命令运行就锁存并保持当前角度。
- 加入三层限位保护：保持目标角度限制在 0–90 度内；输出电压限制在 ±6V；软件限位墙阻断任何把已越限机械臂继续往限位外推的输出。
- 完整保留现有 `moveUp`/`moveDown` 限位检查、按键绑定、Brake 模式和 Elevator 行为。
- 新增 NetworkTables 在线可调保持参数：`/SmartDashboard/Arm Hold kP`、`Arm Hold kD`、`Arm Hold kG`、`Arm FF Offset (deg)`；初值 `kP = 2.0 V/deg`、`kD = 0`、`kG = 0`。
- 在 `periodic()` 中增加角速度估计，并新增遥测量 `Arm/AngleVelocityDegreesPerSecond` 与 `Arm/HoldSetpointDegrees`。
- 修复新测试捕获的符号 bug：`Math.copySign(输出, 标定值)` 会丢弃控制器输出为负时的符号（本会让机械臂在需要回落时反而向上顶住上限位）；改用 `输出 * Math.signum(标定值)`。
- 按队伍要求把机械臂最大电压从 `6V` 降到 `3V`；面板请求的运动电压与位置保持控制器的输出都被限制在 `±3V`。方向逻辑与 0–90 度限位保持不变。
- 修复真机上机械臂到达顶部后晃动的问题：此前的运动命令以恒定 `3V` 一直推到角度限位，机械臂全速到达后在限位墙与饱和的 P 控制器之间来回打摆。
- 将运动与保持统一为同一个位置控制器，设定点以可调速率（默认 `60°/s`）向目标平滑逼近，机械臂接近目标时自然减速，不再撞击限位。
- 增加 `1°` 误差死区在静止时屏蔽比例项，并默认启用微分阻尼（`kD = 0.02 V·s/deg`）抑制超调；新增 `Arm Setpoint Rate (deg/s)` NetworkTables 调节项和 `Arm/HoldTargetDegrees` 遥测。
- 真机测试显示上升途中仍有顿挫后，把硬死区门控改为线性增益渐变：爬升中的跟踪滞后正好落在门控边界上，P 项在零与满输出之间来回跳变。
- 下一轮真机测试后读取 AdvantageScope 实时曲线：上升速度只有 6–8°/s，伴随 1–2°、约 0.5 秒周期的锯齿，顶部有 5–8° 过冲。诊断：60°/s 的设定点斜坡让 P 控制器饱和（3V 撑不住这个爬升速度），量化速度尖峰与粘滑运动失去调节。
- 为速度估计增加低通滤波（α = 0.3）消除编码器量化尖峰对 D 项的干扰，并把默认设定点斜坡速率降到 `30°/s` 让控制器爬升时退出饱和；测试通过后重新部署。
- 后续真机测试反馈上升仍有顿挫，且队伍指出最初的纯电压控制一直很平顺，因此重构了运动策略：运动命令恢复为与最初版本完全相同的开环电压控制（行程中段输出全额请求电压），仅在距离每个限位最后 `15°` 内把电压线性降到 `0.8V`，让机械臂柔和到位。位置控制器保留，但只作为默认保持命令在运动结束后锁存并保持角度。这样既保留队伍验证过的平顺运动，又解决了最初的下坠问题和到顶撞墙抖动。
- 从 AdvantageScope 曲线确认电压驱动的运动双向平滑，但顶部平台停在约 `80°` 并带 1–2° 细密锯齿：双向保持控制器在软件限位墙上来回反弹（推过 90° 被墙清零、回落再推）。
- 用单向迟滞伺服替换保持控制器：允许机械臂自由下垂，低于锁存角度超过 `1°` 时以固定 `0.8V` 轻推回升，回到距锁存点 `0.5°` 内即释放，因此不会冲过锁存点。严格单向的修正不可能与限位墙形成振荡。删除了不再使用的 PID/前馈参数、斜坡机制及对应 NetworkTables 条目；21 个测试全绿，已部署。
- 修复下一轮测试反馈的行程底部抖动：机械臂自然静息位置略低于标定零点，在下限处轻推会与重力陷入"推起-垂落"的死循环。当时改为锁存点距下限 `1°` 以内时保持输出为零，由重力自持。
- 将顶部的迟滞轻推替换为持续重力补偿电压（`HOLD_VOLTAGE`，可在 `/SmartDashboard/Arm Hold Voltage` 在线调节）：轻推的开/关特性天然会在 90° 处产生 ±1° 抖动。锁存角高于 `75°` 时保持输出恒定电压，由软件限位墙在 90° 修剪，全程无切换。
- 下一轮真机测试中机械臂各处不再顿挫，但两端都会缓慢下垂，因此把 `HOLD_VOLTAGE` 从 `1.4V` 提高到 `2.0V`，底部保持从被动静默改为"从下方倚住软件零位"（仅在角度低于 `0°` 时输出持续电压）。两端都主动倚靠软件限位，无切换抖动。28 个测试全绿，已部署。
- 下一轮测试（机器人离线后从 AdvantageScope 缓冲回放）显示下降砸穿零位：减速带仍以 `0.8V` 向下推而重力也在把臂拽向限位，臂最终停在 `-30°`；随后 2.0V 的底部倚零电压把臂猛弹回零位上方，在边界附近来回颠簸。用户同时反馈 90° 处的抖动来自同一种"强电压 + 硬墙"的砰砰开关。
- 三项修复一并落地：下降减速带在限位处降到 `0V`（由重力完成逼近）；底部倚零改用独立的 `0.6V` 轻托；顶部保持电压在 90° 前最后 1° 内线性渐隐到零，使机械臂以零闭合力速度软着陆在限位上、永远不触碰限位墙。默认 `HOLD_VOLTAGE` 提高到 `2.4V`；29 个测试全绿。待机器人上电后部署。
- 通过 AdvantageScope 曲线把顶部残留抖动量化为 ±0.5°、0.5–1 秒周期、停点 85°，判定为编码器量化噪声经 1.2V/度渐隐增益驱动的狩猎；将渐隐带加宽到 2°，并为两个渐隐的输入角度增加 100ms 低通滤波（新增 `Arm/HoldFilteredAngleDegrees` 遥测；安全检查仍使用原始角度）。
- 应队伍测试需要，把 Elevator 运动电压从 `3V` 降到 `1V`，真机反馈后定为 `2V`。
- 新增机械臂–Elevator 联锁：Elevator 命令仅在机械臂到达过 90° 后才会被调度；机械臂在顶部停留期间联锁保持有效，掉回 `80°` 以下即失效。32 个测试全绿；机器人断电循环期间由自动部署任务排队部署。
- 对照队伍的《Arm 与 Elevator 开发》Notion 规格文档做了差距分析，覆盖：双电机闭环架构（电机 1 直驱、电机 2 减速）、可配置保持角度（90°/85°）、以英寸为单位的 Elevator 行程（18–24 in）、through-bore 编码器接入、以及面向 10–25 m 目标的摄像头指向。
- 与队伍确认后，从计划中删除两项需设计加工的硬件（Arm 电机 2 减速传动机构、lead screw 行程确认）；连带从软件清单中移除双闭环代码架构与行程英寸参数化。剩余硬件采购：两只 through-bore 编码器（摄像头为远期）；剩余无硬件依赖的软件项：可配置保持角度与编码器源切换开关。
- 完成两项剩余的无硬件软件项：按键 4 现在升到可配置的 `TARGET_ANGLE_DEGREES`（默认 90°，规格允许 85°）——运动减速带、完成条件、保持渐隐与 Elevator 联锁全部跟随目标角，而 90° 硬限位墙保持不变；同时新增 `ArmIOThroughBore` 与 `USE_THROUGH_BORE_ENCODER` 切换开关（通道/速比/偏移常量为占位值，编码器到货后填入）。33 个测试全绿，含验证 85° 目标下命令正常结束并在硬限位之下解锁联锁的新用例。
- 新增 7 个保持行为测试，覆盖下坠回推、目标角度钳制、上下两侧限位墙、未标定停止和编码器方向；全部 22 个测试与整包构建通过。
- 更新双语 README，补充保持行为、控制公式、调节参数和真机调参步骤。
